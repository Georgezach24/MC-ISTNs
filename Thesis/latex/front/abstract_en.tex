\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\begin{english}

The convergence of terrestrial 5G/6G networks (TN) with Low Earth Orbit
(LEO) satellite networks into Satellite-Terrestrial Integrated Networks
(STINs) is a core design goal of 6G, aiming to provide ubiquitous
coverage beyond the limits of terrestrial infrastructure.
Multi-Connectivity (MC) between terrestrial base stations and satellites
has been proposed as a mechanism to improve reliability, coverage, and
load management in such networks.

This thesis develops a MATLAB simulation framework for a connectivity
scenario consisting of two terrestrial base stations (5G NR, 3GPP TR
38.901 UMa/UMi) and one LEO satellite, in which each user selects its
serving node (terrestrial or satellite) based on the highest SNR (greedy
single-connectivity selection). The model computes propagation loss based
on 3GPP TR 38.901 (with a per-link stochastic LOS/NLOS draw and
log-normal shadow fading) for the terrestrial link, and free-space path
loss (FSPL) with a minimum-elevation visibility mask for the satellite
link, and derives per-user key performance indicators (KPIs): Shannon
capacity, and energy per bit based on a linear power model (the EARTH
model for the base station, a power-amplifier efficiency model for the
satellite).

Beyond the static reference scenario, the thesis extends the simulation
in two directions: (a) repeated runs of the same topology with different
stochastic channel realizations, to characterize KPI variance, and (b)
temporal evolution across multiple consecutive transmissions during a LEO
satellite pass, where the sub-satellite point moves at its real
ground-track speed (derived from the Keplerian orbital period), capturing
how elevation, channel quality, and node handovers evolve over time.
Additionally, a first Monte Carlo framework generates a labeled dataset
over randomized topologies, used for an early proof-of-concept machine
learning model (Logistic Regression, Random Forest) that predicts the
serving node type (terrestrial/satellite) from candidate-link features.

Results show a clear differentiation between terrestrial and satellite
service across the three KPIs examined (throughput, energy per bit, SNR),
with the satellite link offering lower throughput but substantially
better energy efficiency per bit. The temporal pass simulation reveals
two limitations of the current node-selection model that were not
observable in the static scenario: (i) artificially frequent handovers
caused by uncorrelated (i.i.d.) per-timestep shadow fading, and (ii) the
absence of an explicit outage state when no candidate node offers a
usable SNR. These findings define the thesis's ongoing work: a
temporally-correlated shadow-fading model, explicit outage handling, and
extending the machine learning model beyond the current, largely
deterministic argmax(SNR) prediction target toward more realistic
prediction objectives.

\vspace{1em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} multi-connectivity, satellite-terrestrial
integrated networks, 6G, LEO satellites, 3GPP TR 38.901, machine
learning, energy efficiency, handover
